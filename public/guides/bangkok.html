Bangkok travel guide

How to choose a Bangkok neighbourhood, get around and build a balanced itinerary.

Planning note: This is general destination guidance, not live venue or price information. Always verify details with official sources.

Choosing where to stay

Sukhumvit is convenient for restaurants, shopping and access to the BTS Skytrain, but it stretches across a large area. Silom puts you near business districts, parks and evening dining. Riverside hotels can feel quieter and scenic, although trips to other neighbourhoods may take longer. Before booking, check walking distance to a station rather than relying on a broad neighbourhood label.

Getting around

BTS and MRT trains are usually more predictable than road travel during busy periods. Some riverside sights are best approached by boat. Allow extra time for cross-city journeys, especially when travelling to airports or moving between neighbourhoods at rush hour. If using a taxi or ride-hailing service, check the pickup point and route.

Food and evening plans

Bangkok offers everything from street stalls and food courts to destination restaurants. Plan meals around the area you are exploring rather than travelling across town for every stop. For nightlife, distinguish between rooftop bars, live music, night markets and clubs; they have different opening hours, dress expectations and budgets.

A practical three-day outline

Day one: settle in, explore a nearby neighbourhood and try local food. Day two: group historic sights and riverside stops to limit travel time. Day three: explore a market or shopping district, then choose an evening venue close to your accommodation. Keep room for weather and unexpected closures.

Budget and etiquette

Set aside separate budgets for accommodation, transport, attractions and food. Temple visits may require clothing that covers shoulders and knees. Carry water, respect local customs and check venue entry rules. Confirm current opening hours and fees before departure.
