Tokyo travel guide

Plan Tokyo by neighbourhood so you spend less time on trains and more time exploring.

Planning note: This is general destination guidance, not live venue or price information. Always verify details with official sources.

Where to base yourself

Shinjuku and Shibuya offer busy streets and extensive rail connections, while Ueno can suit travellers who prefer museums and a more relaxed base. Tokyo Station is convenient for some intercity journeys. A hotel near a suitable station can matter more than being in the most famous district.

Transport planning

Tokyo has an extensive rail network operated by multiple companies. Check which line serves your destination, allow for station walking distances and avoid assuming that a transfer is immediate. Contactless transit cards can simplify many journeys, but ticket arrangements vary by service.

Organising sightseeing

Choose one or two adjacent areas per day. For example, pair Asakusa with nearby eastern Tokyo sights, or explore Shibuya and Harajuku together. Reserve time for spontaneous stops: small shops, cafés and neighbourhood streets are part of the experience.

Food and reservations

Department-store food halls, casual noodle shops and specialist restaurants offer different price points. Some small venues have limited seating or require reservations. Check dietary requirements in advance and avoid assuming every restaurant accepts every payment method.

Timing and costs

Cherry blossom and autumn foliage periods can bring crowds and higher accommodation demand. Weather varies by season. Compare lodging and transport costs before choosing day trips, and check the latest entry rules for attractions and venues.
